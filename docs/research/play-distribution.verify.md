- GM AAOS 14+ radios (VCUUM) have a USB admission allow-list in system_server that rejects unrecognized USB devices before any app can see them. A USB OBD, CAN or serial adapter plugged into the head unit will likely be refused.: carlink found this by decompiling VCUUM-470.3 services.jar and reproduced it live: UsbHostManager.usbDeviceAdded() rejects unknown device types and shows an 'Unsupported device' toast. The allow-list covers mass storage, MTP/ADB/phone-tablet interfaces, Android Auto's VID/PID pattern, iPhone, GM/Aptiv hubs and generic hubs. The gate is disabled only on debuggable builds, which is why emulators don't show it. carlink's workaround (adapter re-enumeration via UDisk mode with a shared serial) is device-specific and hacky. This matters for the data-source topic, since 'which adapters work' should assume USB adapters attached to the head unit are blocked on the Silverado EV. (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lvalen91/carlink/main/documents/reference/app-notes.md)

- Third-party (untrusted_app) code on GM radios cannot reach GM's proprietary vehicle-data services. Only the standard AAOS CarPropertyManager/VHAL surface is reachable.: gminfo_resources third_party_access.md (gminfo3.7, AAOS 12) says GM's vehiclemanagerservice (IVehicleManagerService.getVehicleData, covering speed, gear, fuel, EV, odometer, temperature, climate, TPMS) is SELinux-labelled gm_vehiclemanager_service and cannot be found by untrusted_app. GM 'prot=normal' permissions such as READ_VEHICLE_STATE are auto-granted but do nothing. GM's platform certificate is not available to third parties (XDA, Jun 2025). This was measured on gminfo3.7, not the EV VCU, but it suggests no GM-private shortcut to BMS data from a Play app. (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lvalen91/gminfo_resources/main/analysis/third_party_access.md https://xdaforums.com/t/general-motors-google-built-in-tinkering.4668105/page-8)
